# Specialist G: adversarial research review

Initial independent review, 11 October 2026. I read the full extracted 43-page manuscript in `tmp/frontier_manuscript.txt`, then checked the current variance summary, claims and confirmation qualification files and the global chemical-pair/state comparison CSV. I did not consult other specialist reports before forming this review. This report distinguishes a proposal worth testing from evidence that it works.

## Decision

The strongest credible next step is a small, explicitly supervised adaptation experiment that asks whether a few matched target-context combinations reveal transferable pair-dependent response structure beyond target calibration. It is reasonable to build the two-coefficient prototype. It is unreasonable to commit to a large context operator, call a two-context success frontier evidence, or interpret failure on the weak TNF-negative panel as proof that biological information is useless.

I disagree with making four bridge labels the default scientific information source without first comparing their benefit with spending the same assays on independent reference precision. Bridge labels reveal treatment outcomes directly and can correct scale or broad attenuation. Opal's latest Base gains already show exactly that explanation can dominate. The strategy earns continuation only by rejecting that simpler explanation.

## What the existing results actually constrain

Verified local artifacts agree with the manuscript's bounded variance conclusion. `results/opal_information_v1/development/summary.json` reports TNF-positive partner MSE 0.0825683915 for variance, 0.0825393157 for capacity-matched means and 0.0825437889 for means. Variance improves 0.231163% over Base but loses to both meaningful controls. TNF-negative partner predictions all equal Base because nested support is unavailable. The file records `information_gate_passed=false`, `confirmation_executed=false`, and zero new independent cultures. This rejects advancement of that representation and search, while leaving other distributions, reliable biological variance and supported target-context adaptation unresolved.

`results/joint-development-20261007/report-v2/comparisons.csv` reports pair-product and mixed gains of -0.870214% and -0.158657% against the selected simple reference. Their conditional intervals include zero. This is adverse evidence for same-information pair kernels on that exposed global pair/whole-state task, not a decisive test of adaptation using new target pair outcomes. The distinction matters because the proposed bridge task supplies an additional supervision tier.

The full manuscript documents count-scale Norman point gains and source-specific routing gains as well as negative primary gates. Describing all residual formulations as disproven would discard these unresolved alternatives. But all these results have been inspected, so further tuning on them cannot serve as fresh confirmation. Leave-SampleIndex-out TNF transfer is also unfavorable to Base versus additivity. A bridge experiment should preserve that failure and report its exact additional target labels.

## Prior-art attack

Single-informed combination modeling and experimental acquisition are established. NAIAD learns adaptive gene embeddings with measured single effects and evaluates optimal combination discovery; its paper reports four CRISPR datasets with more than 350,000 interactions. That establishes close prior work for the scientific idea, not a matched transcriptomic comparison. [Qin et al., ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/qin25g.html).

BATCHIE is a Bayesian sequential design system, including retrospective screens and prospective combination experiments over 206 drugs and 16 cancer lines. Opal cannot claim novelty for choosing informative combinations or a model-agnostic experiment loop; a distinct contribution would need a replicated transcriptomic endpoint and a demonstrated benefit in a specific adaptation regime. [Tosh et al., Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-55287-7).

PertSpectra already combines intervention information and graph-regularized factors to predict unseen observed-single combinations. A low-rank pair basis alone is therefore insufficient novelty. [Author repository](https://github.com/insitro/PertSpectra), [PSB 2026 paper](https://psb.stanford.edu/psb-online/proceedings/psb26/chang_s.pdf).

PIE's official repository describes prediction across unseen contexts and perturbations using pooled response evidence. Its released dataset card lists baseline expression, observed response evidence and several biological knowledge inputs. This makes broad "population-level perturbation effects plus context evidence" novelty especially weak. Verify its exact few-shot double-intervention contract before claiming the proposed bridge task is absent. [PIE repository](https://github.com/ArcInstitute/pie), [released data/model card](https://huggingface.co/arcinstitute/PIE_replogle_wdataset).

TxPert already composes multiple perturbation embeddings with a basal state before decoding effects. CellFlow already models distributions under perturbation screens. Neither proves the exact proposed low-budget bridge contract is solved, but both defeat novelty claims based simply on composition or generating cells. [TxPert paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03113-4), [CellFlow official documentation](https://cellflow.readthedocs.io/en/stable/).

## Conditions that would invalidate the bridge experiment

1. **The query pair exists anywhere in fitting.** Globally held query pair identities must be removed from all source outcomes, all basis fitting, all source selection, all bridge selection and every adaptation fit. Holding out only their target-context row tests transport of a known pair, which is easier and must have a separate name.
2. **The bridge selection uses response strength.** Fix four bridge identities from metadata and permitted single geometry before outcome access, or use a separately registered acquisition algorithm whose selection labels and cost count in the budget. Do not choose bridges after observing the TNF contrast.
3. **Target adaptation learns shared calibration only.** Compare the same four bridge labels with target residual mean, scalar residual shrinkage, constrained affine additivity, source-global calibration and target ridge/kernel adaptation where supported. Give these controls the same gene panel, target singles and bridge outputs. A bridge-supervised operator beating a zero-shot kernel does not identify operator value.
4. **The two operator directions are indistinguishable on bridges.** Compute the singular values of the complete bridge design and sensitivity to dropping each bridge. Nearly collinear columns cannot separately identify the coefficients; refuse the fit under a preregistered condition-number rule and report fallback, rather than treating penalization as identifiability.
5. **The apparent sample size is gene count.** Four pairs across 256 genes remain four intervention outcomes with shared controls, singles and output dependence. Genes help estimate two pooled coefficients under a modeling assumption; they do not justify narrow independent-sample confidence intervals.
6. **The endpoint conceals the desired capability.** Overall mean MSE may improve through shared attenuation while pair-specific residual ranking fails. Require a source-defined signal panel and residual discrimination controls; keep response MSE primary and label the interaction claim separately. With fixed observed additivity, response and residual squared errors are algebraically equal, so reporting both is not independent evidence.
7. **A both-directions gate silently drops unsupported panels.** Freeze support eligibility and fallback counts before responses. If TNF-negative lacks source train support or residual signal, classify the feasibility failure separately from a scientifically powered negative result. Do not substitute the favorable direction after viewing errors.
8. **Two contexts supply a general transfer interval.** TNF-positive and TNF-negative are a single crossed stimulus contrast. A pair/target sensitivity bootstrap can describe this deposit, but context-level generalization and biological uncertainty require more contexts and independent experiments.

## Concrete revisions to the leading proposal

Use a fixed budget of four target bridge pairs and exactly two adaptation coefficients only after metadata confirms at least 36 matched pairs and a common ordered control-selected gene panel. The threshold is an administrative minimum, not a power calculation. Distinguish 32 available non-bridge pairs from the number remaining in each globally held fold. Freeze the exact fold assignments, source training minimum, bridge identities, singular-value rule and fallback behavior before scoring.

Do not choose rank, ridge strength, bridge count, direction or endpoint on target query outcomes. Select source algorithm parameters within source-only folds. If the bridge regularization itself needs tuning, simulate bridge adaptation within source development; never recycle target evaluation as validation. Report the zero-bridge source procedure, four-bridge shared correction and four-bridge operator on identical query pairs. Include a placebo that permutes bridge pairing within the training allowance while preserving pooled target shifts. Include a source operator permutation that keeps target bridge labels but breaks source pair geometry.

For continuation, require material response-error improvement over the best prespecified information-matched bridge reference and residual-discrimination benefit beyond calibration controls, with no unacceptable deterioration on the other direction. A favorable exposed result permits one new independently qualified source or a collaborator pilot. It does not permit repeated rank expansion until a gate is crossed.

The immediate strongest alternative is measurement-aware cross-context response calibration with no claim of learned interactions. If matched four-label calibration matches the operator, retain it as an adaptation reference and spend research effort on a randomized crossed panel that can identify whether context-dependent interactions are reproducible. If even calibration fails despite qualified signal and support, investigate an endpoint-specific scientific ranking task before investing in larger transcriptome prediction.

## Strongest objection

The proposed program may spend months learning a two-parameter assay correction and then seek expensive biological confirmation for a capability that existing compositional systems already provide. The current weak residual reliability and dominant Base calibration gains make this plausible. The defense is an early information-matched calibration comparison and a strict decision to stop the operator when it offers no pair-specific benefit. Ambition should lie in demonstrating a useful low-budget cross-context combination capability with independent evidence, not in treating the operator itself as the scientific contribution.

## Remaining uncertainty

I have not verified that four matched bridges identify two non-collinear source directions on the available pair intersection, that a public deposit supplies independent cultures, or that PIE's native implementation accepts this precise double-intervention few-shot contract. Those are prerequisites to stronger novelty or feasibility claims. This review recommends a decisive development experiment, not a claim that the leading strategy has a quantified probability of frontier success.

## Actual cross-examination and revised verdict

After completing the independent review, I read C's architecture report, E's scientific-utility report and D's evaluation report, then examined the integrated `docs/frontier/experiment_contract.md`. I sent the root concrete criticisms, rather than relying on agreement among authors.

C's original zero-bridge context-contrast proposal requires several source biological contexts or an explicit target supervision route to learn the proposed contrast map. The two available TNF contexts cannot supply both a trained multi-context contrast map and held-context evaluation. C's appended bridge revision resolves that mismatch by fitting only source-defined modes and a tiny target update. I support the revision. Its two coefficients plus an intercept is not a two-parameter fit if the intercept is a freely fitted gene vector; the integrated contract correctly counts and supplies that vector to every shared-calibration comparator. The meaningful capacity restriction concerns pair-dependent target coefficients, not all fitted degrees of freedom.

E's recommended program-ranking use case provides a useful downstream objective, but it can become an unrelated unknown if introduced as another primary endpoint in the first cycle. Freeze a biologically defensible program before scoring or omit its utility claim. An all-gene predictor that improves MSE but leaves the scientist's shortlist unchanged has prediction value without established discovery value. E correctly requires a functional assay for claims beyond transcriptomic program movement and an equal-total-cost randomized campaign for claimed savings.

C and E both require disclosure of held queries sharing no components with bridge pairs. I adopt this as a metadata-defined diagnostic subgroup, with counts frozen before responses. If support is absent, do not redraw bridges to create a favorable subgroup or imply unseen-component transfer. State that the tested adaptation may use shared intervention incidence and require a future partner/component holdout for the broader capability.

D's independent-reference interaction diagnostic resolves the response/residual MSE identity only when prediction and evaluator references are actually separated. If available counts cannot support it, label that diagnostic unavailable. A pair-centered error alone can remove a shared offset but cannot rule out affine or component-incidence corrections. The integrated contract appropriately keeps matched-information calibration controls and response MSE primary.

### Necessary repairs to the integrated contract

The integrated contract is scientifically acceptable for an exposed finite prototype after these implementation details are frozen:

1. **Freeze the identifiability refusal rule.** Reporting condition number is insufficient when the coding agent must decide whether to fit. A proposed conservative numerical rule is effective rank two under relative singular tolerance `1e-8` and condition number at most `1e4`, with fixed fallback otherwise. These are numerical safety rules, not confidence in biological identification. Report leave-one-bridge coefficients and query-prediction sensitivity without selecting a favorable refit.
2. **Freeze scaling and loss normalization.** A fixed penalty of one has different meaning when the number of genes changes or output modes have unequal RMS. Define per-gene averaged squared loss, source-only unit-RMS scaling of the two predicted mode contributions, and persist that scaling. Alternatively specify the exact unnormalized objective and fixed schema; do not let the implementation silently choose. Target query labels cannot determine scales.
3. **State what source-only tuning validates.** With one source biological context in each direction, inner sparse bridge/query folds emulate label-budget adaptation within that source. They do not validate cross-context model selection. This limitation should appear next to the source inner-fold procedure, not only in the broad uncertainty paragraph.
4. **Freeze component-overlap diagnostics.** Record query counts for pairs sharing zero, one or two bridge components before scores. Restrict the headline claim to supported strata and never turn an unsupported diagnostic into an outcome-dependent eligibility rule.

The revised leading strategy is now materially better than the original loosely specified bridge idea. It globally excludes query pairs, locks four bridges, uses the same gene intercept in calibration controls, acknowledges two-context uncertainty and explicitly abandons the operator if calibration matches it. My disagreement remains that an exposed two-context result, even positive, may be mostly an assay-specific correction. Independent crossed cultures are the next scientific investment, and a larger network is conditional on those measurements.

## Final adversarial review after peer revisions

I reviewed the revised integrated `docs/frontier/PLAN.md`, `experiment_contract.md` and `implementation_handoff.md` after the peer cross-examination. The plan incorporates global pair exclusion, matched bridge-informed baselines, source-free and mean-feature controls, numeric rank/conditioning and leave-one-bridge stability rules, independent-reference availability limits, separate public and biological falsification, and a 90-day fallback when laboratory access is absent. This is a coherent execution program, rather than seven disconnected recommendations.

The independent pilot arithmetic is consistent. Twelve identities yield 66 unordered pairs; 40 source pairs plus six candidate bridges plus 20 query pairs partition that inventory. Forty-six nonquery pairs, twelve singles and one control yield 59 conditions per culture, hence 531 conditions across three contexts and three cultures each. Four bridges, 20 queries, twelve singles and one control yield 37 conditions per held-context culture. These are workload quantities before extra controls and replication, not powered sample sizes. Measuring the six candidate bridges in source contexts does not expose the 20 query outcomes. Source-context repetition can qualify measurement precision, but cannot alone establish fresh target-context transfer.

Three final specifications still needed correction when this review was sent to the root:

- The equation in the experiment contract still used an unnormalized squared norm while the root plan promised per-gene averaged loss. Set equal bridge weights explicitly, divide squared error by the frozen gene count, and define source-only unit-RMS mode scaling with a numerical floor. Otherwise a coding agent can implement a different penalty simply by following the equation.
- Specify the source nuisance predictor `g` exactly, including its candidate menu, cross-fitting and selection. Choosing a pair kernel versus a shared/affine reference changes which residual structure enters the proposed source modes, so this is a research decision rather than an implementation detail. Freeze it before the new development run.
- Name the required discrimination endpoint and its pass rule. A generic "prespecified discrimination diagnostic" is insufficient for a machine-readable gate. Use the contracted fixed-pool normalized inverse retrieval rank with a positive paired difference against the locked selector, and record metric qualification failure as inconclusive/no advancement. Do not choose correlation versus retrieval after results.

The actual acquisition ledger should additionally distinguish four bridge identities per evaluated fold from the union of bridge identities across all three folds, and count each target stratum/culture outcome. A cross-validated four-bridge procedure is not a campaign that acquired only four labels in total. This is an accounting clarification, not a reason to change the frozen per-fold budget.

Subject to those exact contract repairs, my final verdict is to execute the bounded prototype and retain the independent crossed measurement program as the only route to a stronger scientific claim. Passing the public administrative gate warrants a measurement investment. Failing with inadequate support stops this prototype spending branch; failing a precise qualified independent useful-margin test stops the proposed biological capability in the tested domain. These are different decisions and the revised plan preserves that distinction.

D's response to this review also preserves a meaningful alternative: spending the same assay budget on independent controls/singles may beat adding bridge labels. Cell subsampling cannot settle that culture-level allocation question. A collaborator pilot should compare equal-total-cost designs if the operator earns advancement. Fixed-policy ranking can proceed without a calibrated uncertainty claim, but uncertainty-guided acquisition still requires its own prospective utility comparison.

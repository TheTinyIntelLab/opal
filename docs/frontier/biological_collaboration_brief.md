# Collaboration brief for review, not sent

Subject: Independent culture/reference precision for perturbation-response prediction

We are developing Opal, an observed-single population-mean perturbation prediction system. Strong reference comparisons have limited its current claims: an exposed crossed-single probe failed its development filter, and a public GxE technical pilot showed residual-half disagreement that did not beat a zero-residual baseline. The software and retained prediction replays work, but we have no independently confirmed useful predictive effect or nominated model.

We seek an assay partner to review whether a small crossed nonquery measurement pilot can identify repeatable pair/context variation and distinguish biological variability from common single/control reference noise. The starting design is six interventions, fifteen pairs and two pretreatment-defined contexts. An independent block contains a pair-outcome culture with its vehicle and two separately initiated reference cultures with six singles/vehicle each: 30 measured condition outcomes, three independent initiations per block. Actual interventions, matching compatibility and culture counts must be chosen by biological review and pilot precision, not by these illustrative counts.

We would need original SOP and reagent/dose/time/vehicle records, independent initiation/parent/plate/well lineage, randomized/blinded assignments, sample-to-count mapping, matching references, QC/exclusion records, complete aggregate/count custody and permitted research/export uses. The lab would supply scientific review, cultures, perturbation and measurements; a biostatistician would review useful margin, covariance, power and finite-sample uncertainty. Opal can supply the information-isolated analysis, simple/kernel controls, state/prediction hashes, replay and resource/failure records. A later conditional confirmation design tests accuracy in fresh cultures of observed contexts, not mechanism or unseen-context transfer.

Please review technical compatibility, independent-reference design, attainable precision, sample/cell/library requirements, available instrument capacity, lead time, staff effort, biosafety/MTA/ethics requirements, data rights and a written currency/itemized quote including QC failures and sequencing. No assay order, funding commitment or collaboration agreement is implied. We have not selected a useful effect size or defensible confirmation sample size; the analytic sensitivity table may make an adequately precise campaign unaffordable. The primary decision is whether a reviewed, finite precision pilot is worth commissioning, with explicit no-go criteria if it cannot resolve a useful signal.

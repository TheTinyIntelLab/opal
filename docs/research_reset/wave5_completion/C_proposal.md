## Hypotheses and falsifiers

H1: Fixed order can be caller invariant while inaccurate under cancellation. Exact rational evaluation of identical binary64 inputs is the accuracy oracle, not a preferred BLAS schedule. Any nonzero fixed-order error falsifies accuracy-as-a-consequence-of-invariance, not the prospective invariance contract.

H2: Frozen scales and coefficients amplify feature error by beta/scale. Prediction discrepancy alone cannot distinguish inaccurate whitening, coefficient-state mismatch, or final cancellation. V2 equal coefficients rule out fitted-state drift for that saved example; v1 needs separate attribution. Signed per-feature reconstruction must agree within a reported rounding residual, while triangle bounds alone cannot establish cause or a floor effect.

H3: Distribution-grounded no-fit synthetic probes can characterize amplification over the saved engineering state without implying biological fidelity. Marginal quantile combinations lose dependence, so use aligned sampled feature tuples and report that limitation explicitly. No outcomes or response headers are needed.

H4: Strict evaluator success requires independent malformed-state and metric oracles. Integer wrap must be prevented, but conversion to float64 is not exact integer arithmetic above 2^53. A valid checkpoint hash establishes byte identity, not scientific provenance.

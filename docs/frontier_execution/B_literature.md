# B primary literature and applicability review

Reviewed 26 October 2026. This is a bounded architecture review using primary papers and official code, not an exhaustive survey or a reproduction. No paper-associated response downloads, notebook output files, response headers or training data were opened. Existing historical result narratives returned aggregate metric tables, as disclosed in B_proposal.md. Published author claims below are distinguished from B's inference. No new numerical result is reported.

## NAIAD is the closest runnable comparator

[Qin et al., ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/qin25g.html) propose measured-single conditioning and adaptive gene embeddings for combinatorial perturbation prediction and acquisition. The paper reports an advantage on four CRISPR datasets; this is an author claim, not Opal validation. The [December 2024 preprint methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12010v2) describe scalar endpoints including fitness, marker enrichment and projected expression signatures. Its downsampling split uses 10% validation and 30% test, so nominal TRAIN count is not total label access. It reports an A100-80G server with 100 GB RAM. Ensemble acquisition uncertainty is not calibrated predictive coverage, and strongest-pair discovery differs from equal-pair MSE. The local degree-dependent embedding idea motivates a small fixed composition, not its large search or active-learning claim.

The [official Apache-2.0 repository](https://github.com/NeptuneBio/NAIAD) is already pinned locally through src/naiad_reference.py to revision 9207e9e855e865160a196a6989392f6db622b0d6, with canonical source hashes and a 100-epoch adapter. The [pinned model implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NeptuneBio/NAIAD/9207e9e855e865160a196a6989392f6db622b0d6/naiad/models.py) sums transformed gene embeddings and adds a learned phenotype head. That head accepts ordered singles, so the local adapter explicitly symmetrizes inference. Gene lookup embeddings need TRAIN support; a random combination split is not evidence for unseen genes. The adapter passes TRAIN/VALIDATION frames and excludes TEST targets. Exact pinned source integrity is essential because current main is not an implementation identity.

B's inference is that the measured-single head is a major competing explanation for apparent interaction gains. Freezing the same validation-selected simple reference for completion, kernel and the small composition makes the incremental comparison clearer. The new I2 deliberately shrinks capacity and changes optimization; call it NAIAD-like, never native NAIAD or a reproduction. The established native comparator remains documented by docs/research_reset/native_results.md and its failed budget gates by label_budget_results.md. Historical full-data native success against calibrated references cannot be inherited by a new low-budget procedure.

## Expression methods do not validate growth prediction

[GEARS, Roohani et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-023-01905-6) predicts transcriptomic outcomes with graph information. The [official MIT-licensed implementation](https://github.com/snap-stanford/GEARS) explicitly says combination prediction needs some combination training data, does not claim cross-cell-type training/transfer support, and uses single-cell expression inputs. B excludes native GEARS here because a scalar growth table plus measured singles does not supply that expression matrix or qualified graphs. Any scalar graph adaptation would be a separate model, requiring feature-matched additive references and a graph ablation. Its knowledge graph must be audited for whether edges encode evaluation-source interaction evidence, and pretrained checkpoints need source-overlap checks.

[CPA's official BSD-3-Clause code](https://github.com/theislab/cpa) describes single-cell latent composition with perturbation, dose, cell-type and batch information. It also supports external embeddings for unseen perturbations. Those are inputs and assumptions, not substitutes for measured local growth singles. No CPA checkpoint, code revision or feature package is qualified for this tournament; native CPA is excluded before execution rather than installed speculatively. Its scalar adaptation would not reproduce the published expression task.

[Systema, Viñas Torné et al., published August 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-025-02777-8), tests whether expression predictors exceed systematic perturbation-control differences. It reports that average-effect references can compete with complex methods and separates perturbation-specific effects from common variation. The [official evaluation repository](https://github.com/mlbio-epfl/systema) implements these comparisons. B's inference is methodological: growth MSE and correlation must be paired with calibrated/main-effect references and technical-confounding worlds. Systema's expression metric is not a growth endpoint or a causal test.

[Ahlmann-Eltze, Huber and Anders, Nature Methods 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02772-6) compare deep perturbation predictions with linear baselines. The [official MIT-licensed code](https://github.com/const-ae/linear_perturbation_prediction-Paper) and its [archived implementation](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14832393) identify the analysis. This supports baseline discipline rather than a claim that a linear growth predictor must win. B did not inspect that repository's source_data or executed notebooks. Direct Nature access failed through its identity-provider redirect and PMC returned a browser challenge, so no detailed split or runtime claim is taken from unavailable full text.

## Recent fitness and graph literature changes what to exclude

[D-LIM, Wang et al., PLOS Computational Biology, March 2026](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014107) models a smooth fitness landscape over independent gene-associated latent phenotypes. It studies mutational variants of a few systems, not arbitrary genome-wide gene knockouts; its discussion explicitly says the genome-wide extension is unresolved. Random 70/30 splits and response-correlation spectral initialization require careful TRAIN-only reconstruction. The paper reports CPU use and low-dimensional successes as well as failure to recover physical-interaction epistasis. The [official MIT-licensed code README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LBiophyEvo/D-LIM-model/main/README.md) documents Linux support and PyTorch dependencies. B excludes native D-LIM because there is no justified mapping of this 60-gene pair task to its gene-specific axes; its value here is motivation for the nonlinear-single world and a strong smooth reference. No fitted latent phenotype can be interpreted biologically from prediction alone.

[Dango, Zhang et al., Cell Systems, June 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2026.101593) predicts higher-order yeast interactions using a hypergraph architecture. The authors claim broad trigenic prediction; it is neither human pair-growth validation nor a singles-only result. The [official code README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ma-compbio/DANGO/master/README.md) names six STRING v9.1 PPI networks, random and gene-based splits, optional protein embeddings, ensemble/Gaussian-process uncertainty and multi-GPU-oriented execution. It estimates about 2 GB GPU memory per model. B excludes it because qualified organism-matched networks, protein features and a GPU resource contract are absent. The repository's prediction filtering and overwrite defaults would also need replacement by full-denominator immutable custody before use. No DANGO data member was opened.

Meta-learning is excluded because the available task has one fitting initiation and no qualified collection of licensed training tasks with independent evaluation-source provenance. Splitting overlapping cohorts into artificial episodes would not create new studies. A future entrant needs an exact task/source inventory, outer study or gene holdouts, local adaptation-label costs and a feature-matched non-meta baseline. No current paper/code examined here establishes that contract for the available inputs. This is an availability decision, not a claim that meta-learning cannot work.

## Shared audit requirements and decision

Every runnable family receives the same actual singles, gene identities, available metadata and local TRAIN/VALIDATION pair labels. Charge validation, preprocessing labels, calibration and early stopping as consulted supervision. Keep single/control measurements separate in the cost ledger. Gene incidence, latent factors and the identity kernel all use TRAIN outcomes incident to TEST genes, so the primary claim is unseen pairs among supported genes. Cold-gene claims require removing every incident label from both TRAIN and VALIDATION, and defining prediction for an untrained identity.

Paper benchmarks do not supply a license or overlap audit for each graph, embedding or pretraining source. Reject held-pair-derived singles, global interaction profiles, full-data normalization, outcome-selected genes and pretrained source overlap before comparison, or register them as a different exposed task. TRAIN-fitted moments/centers and common support/fallback reporting keep stronger input access from masquerading as architecture improvement.

The finite menu in B_proposal.md therefore retains calibrated singles, gene main effects, flexible singles, rank-eight completion, an identity-plus-single symmetric tensor kernel, and a small regularized residual composition. Only the native historical NAIAD comparator has an exact external execution pin here; excluded methods are literature context and no mutable main URL is an approved executable revision. Root's new code needs its own freeze, transitive hashes, profiling and replay. Neither literature nor synthetic recovery establishes a positive biological result.

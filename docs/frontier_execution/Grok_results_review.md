## What the six rows are

Every fold charged 16,000 train and 4,000 validation labels, with `replaced_rows` 0. Every fold's full test count is at most 7,343, under the 10,000 per-procedure ceiling, and `two_partner_test` equals `full_test`. Unsupported charged validation rows are 0. The pooled test counts are the sums of five disjoint within-fold test blocks: the scorer's unique-pair check passed, and those sums equal the pooled counts in `results.json`.

The test blocks are disjoint. The training labels are not five independent samples. `block_assignments` marks a pair as test only when both genes fall in that fold. In every other fold that pair remains eligible for the train or validation hash prefix. This review did not open pair-index arrays, so the size of that reuse is not measured here. The thirty procedures are thirty fits on overlapping cross-fold labels, not thirty cohorts.

Horlbeck K562 and Jurkat are one pair partition and two phenotypes. The loader copies one gene array and one pair array into both contexts, both use accession `GSE116198` in the budget and validation hashes, and the fold test counts match exactly: 2,970, 2,970, 3,110, 3,948, 3,212. Both drop 40 genes that sit in the 467-name bank list and in no eligible pair, leaving 427 genes, and all 427 appear in the pooled test. The selected policies have the same name. The selected simples do not: K562 uses `B2--alpha10` and clears 5%; Jurkat uses `B3--nodes1-alpha10` and does not. The 5% denominator is each task's own validation-selected simple. This review did not replace Jurkat's baseline.

Environment1 and environment2 have the same accession salt, `GSE312636`, because the salt omits the task name. Their fold test counts match exactly: 1,711, 1,540, 2,278, 1,378, 2,926. Both collapse to 313 genes with nothing dropped, and all 313 appear in the test. The banks were not opened, so pair-name identity was not compared. A shared gene-name roster would produce this identity; the counts alone are what this review saw.

PARP 2022 and PARP 2023 share accession `GSE284018` and do not share a roster. Fold counts differ, collapsed genes are 539 and 537, and dropped unused genes are 4 and 6. They are two processed banks from one accession family, not one split and not an untouched confirmation source.

Pooled MSE weights pairs, so larger blocks weigh more. The largest environment block has 2,926 of 9,833 test pairs; the largest PARP 2022 block has 7,343 of 29,016; the largest Horlbeck block has 3,948 of 16,210. `results.json` stores no per-fold MSE. Jurkat's 4.11% is a pooled number. The leave-one-gene map has no nonnegative mean, and that map is not a leave-one-block check. A block can sit on the other side of 5%, or of zero, without appearing in the task reason list.

## Selection and the two gain questions

Validation chooses the simple, the completion checkpoint, and the family, all on the same charged validation labels. There is no third split. `ScalarCompletion` restores the validation-best matrix on the 25-step grid. Across the thirty `I0` records, `best_step` runs from 25 to 500; seven folds stop at 25 and three run to 500. The menu then ranks families by validation MSE, which for `I0` is computed on that already chosen checkpoint. Ridge, kernel, and the neural net do not get that inner loop. The neural fit is a fixed step count. Every nominated policy is still `I0--rank8-penalty0.01`. On this ledger that same series also has the lowest reported test mean MSE, so the validation double use did not pick a test-worse family. The validation MSE itself remains an optimistic ranking for completion.

The effect-size gate and the gene interval are different functionals. The gate is the equal-pair relative reduction `(simple MSE - policy MSE) / simple MSE`, failed when that ratio is below 0.05. The interval is the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles of a 2,000-draw gene-multiplicity bootstrap of the absolute paired squared-error difference, seed 20261022, as implemented by `paired_gene_uncertainty`. The results object records 2,000 nonempty draws and zero discarded draws. It does not repeat the seed; `protocol.json` does. Jurkat's interval lies below zero while the relative reduction stays under 5%. A negative upper endpoint does not clear the effect-size gate.

The infection clause is a one-sided point comparison on the same pairs: fail only when policy infection MSE is greater than the selected simple. Both infections improved on every task, including Jurkat. They are two dependent assay columns of one experiment. The clause does not demand a 5% infection gain.

The shuffle veto is strict: a tie with a validation-selected shuffled policy fails. The stored permutable fraction is 1.0 on every fold, so the frozen control moved every training row. The control itself is one cyclic shift inside each of four median strata, three fixed seeds, not a draw from the exchangeable residual permutations. Beating those three policies is the registered veto. It is not a max over permutations. Jurkat clears that veto and still fails the program.

Screen singles are inputs to every fold. The fold holdout does not hold out singles. Horlbeck uses the context-specific mean of the bank single array. External screens average the two single-infection columns after the loader's eligibility mask. That mask can drop external pairs for nonfinite pair targets or low counts before the hash split. Horlbeck's loader does not apply it; it checks the historical 81,466-pair array. The growth accounting records unused-gene drops and does not record the external pair-exclusion count. `label_budget.supported_queries`, which uses degree above zero and drops unsupported test rows, is not this gate. This gate uses two distinct training partners and keeps the full test. In this run the distinction is idle: every test pair was supported.

`fit_procedure` writes one support mask, taken from the last persisted model, and the scorer applies that mask to every family. This menu builds every model with the same degree-at-least-two support, and this run's mask covers every test pair. A later model with a different support vector would be scored on the wrong rows without a per-model check. Replay compares reloaded predictions with `numpy.array_equal` and discards the reloaded support mask.
